Three words to remember

PRIOR

What we believed before.

LIKELIHOOD

How compatible the evidence is with different possibilities.

POSTERIOR

What we believe after incorporating the evidence.

Now the equation

Bayes' theorem

P(H|E) = P(E|H) · P(H)P(E)

The equation formalizes the process we just experienced.

Two ways to learn from data

Frequentist vs. Bayesian

Frequentist

Assume there is a fixed true value. Use repeated observations to estimate it.

H H T H T H H T → 62.5%

Bayesian

Represent uncertainty directly. Update the distribution when new evidence arrives.

prior → evidence → posterior

Both are useful. They answer uncertainty in different ways.

Important distinction

Modern AI is more complicated than today's equation.

Neural networks and large language models are not simply applying Bayes' theorem once per prediction. But probability, conditional information, uncertainty, and learning from data are foundational ideas across AI.

Final challenge

An AI says: “There is an 85% chance this is true.”

What should you want to know before trusting it?

What data did it see? How much data? What assumptions are built in? How uncertain is it? What evidence would change its answer?
Takeaway

Intelligence is knowing how to reason when you are uncertain.

Probability gives us the language. Bayesian inference gives us a way to update. AI scales those ideas with data and computation.
